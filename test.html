We also tested framing across these themes

Household Parity

The phrase "unpaid care work" triggered the highest defensiveness. Reframing the concept as "peace and prosperity" neutralized the threat and secured male agreement.

Paternity Leave

Transactional language ("split the responsibility") feels like an administrative chore. Relational language ("essential time to bond") appeals directly to the modern father's desire for emotional presence.

Gender Norms

Men perceive "outdated gender norms" as an attack on their cultural heritage, but unanimously agree that their ultimate goal is ensuring "children thrive."

Marital Roles

Avoids zero-sum independence dynamics, framing shared care as joint resilience and mutual teamwork.

Workplace Norms

Replaces gender competition with universal employee well-being, driving corporate acceptability.

Presence has a cost

Beyond message framing, shared caregiving demands time.

This creates time constraints, especially for dual-income households. This is often managed by outsourcing domestic chores.

What needs to change?

Message Reframing

Shift public advocacy away from polarizing terms such as “dismantling the patriarchy,” and focus entirely on child developmental outcomes, household peace, and shared economic resilience.

Build Pressure on Policy

Expand paid paternity leave beyond the 14-day public benchmark to the private sector, and establish community childcare for informal workers.

Reshape Norms and Socialization

Form alliances with religious and traditional gatekeepers to preach sermon centered on mutual submission, financial transparency, and sacrificial, tenderhearted marital partnership.
